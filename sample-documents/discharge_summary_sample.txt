MEDICATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────

Please take the following medications as instructed. Do not take more than the stated dose.

1. Ibuprofen 400 mg
   - Take ONE tablet (400 mg) every 6 hours as needed for pain.
   - Always take with food or milk to protect your stomach.
   - Do not take if you have stomach problems or are allergic to ibuprofen.
   - Duration: up to 5 days. Stop sooner if pain resolves.

2. Paracetamol 500 mg
   - Take TWO tablets (1000 mg) every 6 hours as needed.
   - Do not take more than 8 tablets (4000 mg) in 24 hours.
   - Duration: as needed, up to 2 weeks.

3. Omeprazole 20 mg (stomach protection)
   - Take ONE capsule each morning, 30 minutes before breakfast.
   - Take for as long as you are taking Ibuprofen.
   - Duration: up to 5 days (matches Ibuprofen course).

You may take Ibuprofen and Paracetamol together for stronger pain control — they work in different ways.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────
ACTIVITY RESTRICTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────

Please follow these restrictions to support your recovery and avoid complications:

  • DO NOT DRIVE for at least 7 days after your procedure, and only when you are confident you can perform an emergency stop safely. Check with your insurer if unsure.
  • DO NOT bear full weight on your right leg for the first 3 days. Use the crutches provided. You may gently put weight down when sitting.
  • DO NOT climb stairs unaided for the first 3 days. If you must use stairs, use the crutches and go one step at a time.
  • DO NOT return to work for at least 7 days (office/sedentary role) or at least 6 weeks (physical/manual role). Discuss your specific role with your GP if uncertain.
  • DO NOT swim or submerge the knee in water (baths, pools, hot tubs) until the wound is fully healed — typically 2 weeks.
  • You may shower carefully from tomorrow. Keep the dressing dry. Cover the knee with a waterproof dressing if available.
  • Gentle walking with crutches is encouraged from Day 1 to maintain circulation.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────
WARNING SIGNS — WHEN TO SEEK HELP
─────────────────────────────────────────────────

Most recoveries from arthroscopic knee surgery are straightforward. However, you should contact your GP or return to the hospital if you notice any of the following:

  • Fever above 38°C (100.4°F) — a thermometer reading, not just feeling warm
  • Increasing redness, warmth, or swelling around the incision site (some swelling is normal in the first 48 hours, but it should not be getting worse after that)
  • Discharge or oozing from the wound that is green, yellow, or foul-smelling
  • Severe pain not controlled by the prescribed medications
  • Numbness or pins and needles in the foot or lower leg that is new or worsening
  • Significant swelling of the calf (lower leg) — this may indicate a blood clot (DVT)
  • Redness or pain that is spreading up or down the leg from the knee

CALL 999 OR GO TO A&E IMMEDIATELY if you experience:
  • Sudden chest pain or difficulty breathing (this may indicate a clot in the lung)
